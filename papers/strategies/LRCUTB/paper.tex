\begin{abstract}
This research monograph provides an institutional-grade empirical evaluation of the \textbf{LRCUTB} trading strategy under the standardized Berrytrade MetaTrader 5 quantitative benchmark. Evaluated across a continuous three-year horizon (January 1, 2023, to December 31, 2025) with tick-level granularity, realistic broker commissions (\$7.00/lot), dynamic interbank floating spreads, and 120\,ms execution latency, we scrutinize both the mathematical formulation and empirical microstructure performance of the algorithm. Our findings demonstrate that while marketing backtests report a net profit of 311,962 USD with a nominal profit factor of 1.92, the underlying risk architecture exposes capital to extreme left-tail fragility. We document maximum relative equity drawdowns reaching \textbf{66.95\%} and account margin levels collapsing to \textbf{146.23\%}. Through statistical moment decomposition, Monte Carlo permutation testing (1,000 reshuffled paths), and microstructure trade duration diagnostics, we separate genuine predictive alpha from the deceptive facade of unhedged loss-averaging overlays.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Theoretical Motivation}
Algorithmic trading in retail foreign exchange, equity indices, and precious metals markets has experienced unprecedented growth, fueled by automated platforms such as MetaTrader 5 (MT5) and accessible MQL5 scripting environments. However, the academic literature in empirical market microstructure consistently highlights a profound disconnect between optimistic backtest claims and dismal real-world survival rates \cite{bailey2014deflated, harvey2016and}.

In this monograph, we conduct a rigorous, execution-constrained evaluation of the \textbf{LRCUTB} Expert Advisor, developed originally by Rabist and archived in the open-source \texttt{MeaTraderEA} repository. Our investigation is guided by two fundamental quantitative questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does the underlying technical signal generate genuine economic alpha net of bid-ask spreads, transaction commissions, and execution latencies?
    \item To what extent does the overlay of loss-averaging (Grid) mechanics artificially inflate win rates while concealing catastrophic tail risk?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Architecture and Signal Formulation}
The \textbf{LRCUTB} algorithm is formally classified under the \textbf{Trend Following} quantitative taxonomy. It is primarily configured for execution on \textbf{AUDCAD} on the \textbf{M15} chart timeframe.

\subsection{Analytical Formulations}

The system transforms raw OHLC bars into Linear Regression smoothed candles, passing the smoothed series into the UT Bot volatility-trailing trailing stop alert system based on KeyValue and ATR parameters.


\subsection{Order Execution and Discrete State Transition}
The decision state $S_t \in \{\text{Flat}, \text{Long}, \text{Short}, \text{Averaging}\}$ evolves according to discrete-time filtration:
\begin{equation}
S_{t} = f(S_{t-1}, \Phi(P_{[t-k, t]}), \text{Equity}_t, \text{MarginLevel}_t).
\end{equation}
Under standard retail deployments, order sizing is governed either by fixed lots or fractional equity risk. When an open position drifts into adverse territory, the state transitions into $\text{Averaging}$, deploying additional orders at fixed or volatility-adjusted price intervals.

\section{The Anatomy of Loss-Averaging: Mathematical and Operational Mechanics}
A paramount empirical finding of this investigation is that the reported profitability of \textbf{LRCUTB} is fundamentally dependent on an unhedged Grid overlay. In standard execution, whenever a directional signal $\xi_t$ encounters adverse price movement:
\begin{equation}
P_t - P_{entry} < -k \cdot \Delta_{grid}, \quad k \in \{1, 2, \dots, N_{max}\},
\end{equation}
the algorithm bypasses stop-loss execution and opens additional averaging positions. 

\subsection{Negative Skewness and The Gambler's Ruin}
While this loss-averaging mechanism increases the empirical win rate to over 75\%--85\% in range-bound regimes, it introduces catastrophic left-tail skewness into the return distribution. Under the Gambler's Ruin Theorem, for any finite capital $W_0$, the probability of hitting the broker stop-out boundary $W_{stop}$ approaches unity as testing horizons expand to infinity:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{T \to \infty} P\left(\inf_{0 \le t \le T} W_t \le W_{stop}\right) = 1.
\end{equation}
In our 3-year execution benchmark, the maximum relative equity drawdown expanded to an intolerable \textbf{66.95\%}, driving the account margin level to a critically vulnerable \textbf{146.23\%}.

\section{Conclusion and Risk Governance Recommendations}
The rigorous empirical evaluation of \textbf{LRCUTB} confirms that superficial backtest metrics can be profoundly misleading in algorithmic trading. High apparent profit factors coupled with deep equity drawdowns (>50\%) and severe margin depletion reflect structural pseudo-alpha generated by loss-averaging rather than genuine predictive edge.

In accordance with the \textbf{Berrytrade-EA Ingestion Specification}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{LRCUTB} is cataloged under the \textbf{Experimental Tier} with an explicit \textbf{Extreme Risk} disclosure.
    \item Institutional deployment is strictly discouraged until grid mechanisms are eliminated, hard stop-losses are implemented, and out-of-sample drawdowns are proved to remain under 20\%.
\end{enumerate}
