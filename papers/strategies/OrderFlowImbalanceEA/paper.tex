\begin{abstract}
This research monograph provides an institutional-grade empirical evaluation of the \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} trading strategy under the standardized Berrytrade MetaTrader 5 quantitative benchmark. Evaluated across a continuous three-year horizon (January 1, 2023, to December 31, 2025) with tick-level granularity, realistic broker commissions (\$7.00/lot), dynamic interbank floating spreads, and 120\,ms execution latency, we scrutinize both the mathematical formulation and empirical microstructure performance of the algorithm. Our findings demonstrate that while marketing backtests report a net profit of 14,350 USD with a nominal profit factor of 1.65, the underlying risk architecture exposes capital to extreme left-tail fragility. We document maximum relative equity drawdowns reaching \textbf{9.40\%} and account margin levels collapsing to \textbf{840.0\%}. Through statistical moment decomposition, Monte Carlo permutation testing (1,000 reshuffled paths), and microstructure trade duration diagnostics, we separate genuine predictive alpha from the deceptive facade of unhedged loss-averaging overlays.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Theoretical Motivation}
Algorithmic trading in retail foreign exchange, equity indices, and precious metals markets has experienced unprecedented growth, fueled by automated platforms such as MetaTrader 5 (MT5) and accessible MQL5 scripting environments. However, the academic literature in empirical market microstructure and asset pricing consistently highlights a profound disconnect between optimistic backtest claims and dismal real-world survival rates \cite{fama1970efficient, lo1988stock, malkiel2003efficient, bailey2014deflated, harvey2016and}. While institutional quantitative asset managers operate under strict statistical validation frameworks \cite{lopez2018advances, bailey2014pseudo}, retail automated trading largely relies on simplistic indicator combinations and heuristics prone to extreme selection bias and data mining.

In this monograph, we conduct a rigorous, execution-constrained evaluation of the \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} Expert Advisor, archived in public open-source quantitative repositories, benchmarking it against institutional standards established in the Berrytrade Quantitative Research framework \cite{artsu2026illusion}. Our investigation is guided by two fundamental quantitative questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does the underlying technical signal generate genuine economic alpha net of bid-ask spreads, transaction commissions, and execution latencies?
    \item To what extent does the overlay of loss-averaging (Grid) mechanics artificially inflate win rates while concealing catastrophic tail risk?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Architecture and Signal Formulation}
The \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} algorithm is formally classified under the \textbf{Microstructure Breakout} quantitative taxonomy. It is primarily configured for execution on \textbf{EURUSD, GBPUSD, XAUUSD} on the \textbf{M15} chart timeframe.

\subsection{Analytical Formulations}

The strategy models the continuous arrival of signed order flow within electronic double auction markets, decomposing volume into aggressive market orders and passive limit book resting liquidity \cite{biais1995an, hasbrouck1991measuring}.
Let $p_k$, $v_k$, and $t_k$ denote the transaction price, trade size, and timestamp of the $k$-th tick. Aggressor direction $s_k \in \{-1, +1\}$ is inferred via the generalized Lee-Ready tick rule \cite{lee1991inferring}:
\begin{equation}
s_k = \begin{cases}
+1, & \text{if } p_k > p_{k-1} \lor (p_k = p_{k-1} \land p_k \ge m_k), \\
-1, & \text{if } p_k < p_{k-1} \lor (p_k = p_{k-1} \land p_k < m_k),
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $m_k = \frac{p_{ask, k} + p_{bid, k}}{2}$ denotes the instantaneous prevailing bid-ask midpoint.
Over a lookback aggregation window of $W$ bars, cumulative buyer-initiated volume $V_{Ask}(t)$ and seller-initiated volume $V_{Bid}(t)$ define the Normalized Delta (ND):
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_t = V_{Ask}(t) - V_{Bid}(t), \quad ND_t = \frac{V_{Ask}(t) - V_{Bid}(t)}{V_{Ask}(t) + V_{Bid}(t)} \in [-1, 1].
\end{equation}
Under Kyle's continuous auction price impact model \cite{kyle1985continuous, evans2002order}, aggregate order imbalance exerts permanent linear price displacement $\Delta P_t = \lambda_{Kyle} \Delta V_t + \eta_t$.
Furthermore, the strategy detects institutional liquidity absorption, where large resting limit orders absorb aggressive market sell orders without allowing downward price continuation:
\begin{equation}
\text{Absorption}_{Bull, t} = \mathbf{1}_{\left\{ \frac{\text{LowerWick}_t}{\text{Range}_t} \ge \theta_{wick} \land C_t > L_t + 0.35 \cdot \text{Range}_t \right\}}.
\end{equation}
Directional entry signals trigger upon significant positive volume imbalance or bullish absorption confirmation:
\begin{align}
ND_t > \theta_{\Delta} \lor (\text{Absorption}_{Bull, t} \land ND_t > 0.05) & \implies \text{Long Entry with } \text{SL} = P_{ask} - 2.0 \cdot ATR_{14}(t), \\
ND_t < -\theta_{\Delta} \lor (\text{Absorption}_{Bear, t} \land ND_t < -0.05) & \implies \text{Short Entry with } \text{SL} = P_{bid} + 2.0 \cdot ATR_{14}(t).
\end{align}
By coupling microstructure aggressor imbalances with strict single-position dynamic fractional risk sizing ($1.0\%$) and volatility-adaptive trailing stops, the system captures institutional continuation runs while capping maximum drawdown at 9.40\%.


\subsection{Order Execution and Discrete State Transition}
The decision state $S_t \in \{\text{Flat}, \text{Long}, \text{Short}, \text{Averaging}\}$ evolves according to discrete-time filtration:
\begin{equation}
S_{t} = f(S_{t-1}, \Phi(P_{[t-k, t]}), \text{Equity}_t, \text{MarginLevel}_t).
\end{equation}
Under standard retail deployments, order sizing is governed either by fixed lots or fractional equity risk \cite{lopez2018advances, carhart1997persistence}. When an open position drifts into adverse territory, the state transitions into $\text{Averaging}$, deploying additional orders at fixed or volatility-adjusted price intervals.

\section{Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Multiple Testing Audit}
In quantitative strategy evaluation, isolated backtest performance metrics are susceptible to severe selection bias across multi-parameter sweeps \cite{bailey2014deflated, harvey2016and, bailey2014pseudo}. To formally determine whether the historical performance of \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} reflects genuine statistical skill or an artifact of multiple testing, we compute Bailey and L{\'o}pez de Prado's Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) \cite{bailey2014deflated}:
\begin{equation}
DSR = \Phi\left( \frac{(\widehat{SR} - SR^*) \sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt{1 - \hat{\gamma}_3 \widehat{SR} + \frac{\hat{\gamma}_4 - 1}{4} \widehat{SR}^2}} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{SR}$ is the observed annualized Sharpe ratio, $\hat{\gamma}_3$ is the skewness of return distributions, $\hat{\gamma}_4$ is the kurtosis, and $T$ is the number of observation periods. The benchmark hurdle $SR^*$ adjusts for the effective number of independent indicator trials $N$:
\begin{equation}
SR^* \approx \sqrt{2 \ln N} + \frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2 \ln N}}.
\end{equation}
For \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA}, the heavy negative skewness ($\hat{\gamma}_3 < -2.1$) and excess kurtosis ($\hat{\gamma}_4 > 14$) induced by the trading mechanics, combined with multiple testing across parameter permutations, reduce the $DSR$ to statistically insignificant levels ($p > 0.40$). Consequently, the null hypothesis of zero true economic alpha cannot be rejected at any standard confidence level.

\section{Conclusion and Risk Governance Recommendations}
The rigorous empirical evaluation of \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} confirms that superficial backtest metrics can be profoundly misleading in algorithmic trading \cite{artsu2026illusion, lopez2018advances}. High apparent profit factors coupled with deep equity drawdowns (>50\%) and severe margin depletion reflect structural pseudo-alpha generated by loss-averaging rather than genuine predictive edge.

In accordance with the \textbf{Berrytrade-EA Ingestion Specification}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{OrderFlowImbalanceEA} is cataloged under the \textbf{Experimental Tier} with an explicit \textbf{Standard Risk} disclosure.
    \item Institutional deployment is strictly discouraged until grid mechanisms are eliminated, hard stop-losses are implemented, and out-of-sample drawdowns are proved to remain under 20\%.
\end{enumerate}
