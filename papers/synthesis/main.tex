\begin{abstract}
Retail algorithmic trading ecosystems have witnessed an explosive proliferation of automated Expert Advisors (EAs) marketed as institutional-grade alpha engines. This paper conducts an exhaustive empirical case study auditing nineteen distinct MetaTrader 5 trading strategies---comprising ten popular indicator-driven grid systems and nine disciplined non-grid benchmark architectures. Utilizing authentic tick-by-tick historical trade logs and real market price series spanning 2020 to 2026 under institutional execution frictions (\$7.00/lot round-turn commissions, dynamic floating spreads, and 120\,ms latency), we dissect popular retail technical extractors including Bollinger Bands, RSI, MACD, Moving Averages, and the Andean Oscillator.

To resolve longstanding debates regarding the economic source of retail trading profits, we conduct a controlled, paired four-arm ablation experiment on identical historical gold quotes (\texttt{XAUUSD}). We show that: (1) standalone indicator signals exhibit sub-break-even economic expectation (in-sample Profit Factor 0.91, out-of-sample 0.42); (2) unhedged grid overlays artificially manufacture high win rates ($>$80\% to 100\%) and high in-sample Profit Factors (up to 99.0) even when fed completely pseudo-random coin-toss entries; and (3) this manufactured profitability is entirely an artifact of severe negative return skewness and massive left-tail capital absorption, culminating in catastrophic forced liquidation (up to 93.44\% to 101.36\% drawdown) during sustained directional trends such as the historic 2024 gold expansion. 

We reconcile original author-reported backtest baselines against standardized multi-year institutional audits. We derive the exact first hitting time of absorbing margin boundaries under geometric grid expansion, formalize the impossibility of alpha generation via Doob's Optional Stopping Theorem on Supermartingales, and compute the Bailey and L{\'o}pez de Prado Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) under severe non-normality. Finally, we introduce the \textbf{Berrytrade Two-Tier Governance Framework} as an open-source institutional protocol designed to eliminate deceptive pseudo-alpha from quantitative trading repositories.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A foundational debate in empirical asset pricing and market microstructure centers on whether publicly available technical signals can generate abnormal risk-adjusted returns net of real-world execution frictions \cite{fama1970efficient, lo1988stock, jegadeesh1993returns, carhart1997persistence}. While institutional quantitative asset managers operate under strict statistical validation frameworks designed to control for selection bias and backtest overfitting \cite{bailey2014deflated, harvey2016and, lopez2018advances}, the retail algorithmic trading ecosystem largely resides in an unregulated domain dominated by social media marketing and unreproducible backtest claims.

Retail trading platforms, foremost among them MetaTrader 5 (MT5), have democratized algorithmic execution through the MQL5 object-oriented programming language. Millions of retail traders deploy commercial and open-source Expert Advisors (EAs) across spot foreign exchange and precious metals (notably Gold, \texttt{XAUUSD}). However, empirical survival rates among retail accounts remain notoriously low \cite{bouchaud2003theory}. 

A prominent case study is provided by the open-source \texttt{MeaTraderEA} strategy collection, which offers diverse technical methodologies spanning Zero-Lag SMAs, Williams Fractals, Nadaraya-Watson Kernel Envelopes, and Commitment of Traders (COT) sentiment indicators. Yet, embedded within each strategy's technical documentation is an identical and revealing confession:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Note: The pure implementation of the strategy was not profitable over the long term. The current implementation combines this strategy with the Grid technique, which has made it profitable.''}
\end{quote}

This admission raises critical research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does the combination of technical indicators with grid overlays constitute genuine statistical alpha, or is it merely an engineering sleight-of-hand that trades left-tail ruin for inflated short-term win rates?
    \item When evaluated under realistic execution frictions and regime transitions, what are the exact empirical loss distributions and margin depletion trajectories of these retail architectures?
    \item Can an institutional governance protocol objectively filter out pseudo-alpha while preserving genuinely robust quantitative models?
\end{enumerate}

This monograph presents a comprehensive empirical and mathematical examination addressing these questions. We analyze nineteen distinct MQL5 strategies using authentic trade records parsed directly from Strategy Tester deal logs and five-year historical market price series, eliminating all synthetic or parametric approximations.

\section{Mathematical Foundations of Technical Extractors vs. Loss-Averaging}

\subsection{Asset Price Dynamics and Discrete Filtering}
Let $P_t$ denote the mid-price of an asset governed by a continuous-time jump-diffusion semimartingale:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dP_t}{P_{t^-}} = \mu dt + \sigma dW_t + J_t dN_t,
\end{equation}
where $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion, $N_t$ is a Poisson jump counter with intensity $\lambda_J$, and $J_t$ is the jump magnitude distribution. 

In discrete sampling intervals $\Delta t$, a technical indicator functions as a linear or non-linear filter $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}$. A trading rule maps filter outputs to a target position:
\begin{equation}
\xi_t = \text{sign}(\Phi(P_{t}, P_{t-1}, \dots, P_{t-k}) - \theta), \quad \xi_t \in \{-1, 0, 1\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Microstructure Frictions: Spread, Slippage, and Broker Commissions}
In institutional execution modeling, market orders cross the half-spread and incur execution delay:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{exec} = P_t \pm \frac{S_t}{2} + \lambda_{Kyle} \cdot V_t + \epsilon_{\tau},
\end{equation}
where $S_t$ is the instantaneous spread, $\lambda_{Kyle}$ represents adverse market impact \cite{kyle1985continuous}, and $\epsilon_{\tau}$ denotes slippage accumulated during the execution delay $\tau = 120\,\text{ms}$. 

Let $R_t = \xi_{t-1} \frac{\Delta P_t}{P_{t-1}}$ be the gross strategy return. The net return process is given by:
\begin{equation}
R_{net, t} = R_t - \frac{|\Delta \xi_t|}{2} \left( \frac{S_t}{P_t} + \frac{C_{rt}}{P_t \cdot M_c} + \frac{2\epsilon_{\tau}}{P_t} \right),
\end{equation}
where $C_{rt} = \$7.00$ per round-turn standard lot (\$3.50 half-turn commission per trade side), and $M_c$ denotes symbol contract size (e.g., 100,000 for FX, 100 for Gold). For high-frequency signals on M5 or M15 timeframes, the drag of the friction term $\frac{|\Delta \xi_t|}{2} (\dots)$ rapidly degrades the expectation $\mathbb{E}[R_{net, t}]$ from weakly positive to strictly negative.

\subsection{Theorem 1: First Hitting Time of Margin Boundary under Geometric Grid Overlays}
To mask this negative expectation $\mathbb{E}[R_{net, t}] < 0$, retail developers superimpose a Grid execution overlay.

\textbf{Definition (Geometric Grid Overlay):} Let the entry price of an initial position be $P_0$ with lot size $V_0$. If the market price moves adversely against the position by multiples of grid spacing $\delta > 0$, additional averaging orders are opened at discrete price levels $P_k = P_0 - k \cdot \delta$ with lot sizes $V_k = V_0 \cdot \gamma^k$, where $\gamma \ge 1$ is the volume multiplier ($\gamma = 1$ denotes an arithmetic grid; $\gamma > 1$ denotes a geometric or Martingale grid).

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Margin Exhaustion and Inevitable Ruin):} \textit{Let account equity be bounded by initial capital $W_0 < \infty$. For any unhedged grid with spacing $\delta > 0$ and multiplier $\gamma \ge 1$ operating on an asset with non-zero volatility $\sigma > 0$, the cumulative capital absorption $\Omega(K)$ required to maintain a grid of depth $K$ grows as:}
\begin{equation}
\Omega(K) = L(K) + M_{req}(K),
\end{equation}
\textit{where floating open loss $L(K)$ and maintenance margin requirement $M_{req}(K)$ satisfy:}
\begin{align}
L(K) &= M_c \sum_{j=0}^{K-1} V_j (P_j - P_K) = M_c \delta V_0 \sum_{j=0}^{K-1} \gamma^j (K - j), \\
M_{req}(K) &= m \sum_{j=0}^K V_j = m V_0 \left( \frac{\gamma^{K+1}-1}{\gamma-1} \right) \quad (\text{for } \gamma > 1),
\end{align}
\textit{where $m$ is the initial margin requirement per standard lot. Consequently, $\Omega(K) \in \mathcal{O}(\gamma^K)$. The first hitting time of the broker stop-out boundary $\tau_{ruin} = \inf \{t > 0 : W_t \le \alpha_{so} M_{req}(K(t))\}$ satisfies:}
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ruin} < \infty) = 1,
\end{equation}
\textit{for any asset price path whose maximum excursion $\sup_{0 \le t \le T} |P_t - P_0|$ exceeds the critical absorption threshold $K^* \delta$, where $K^* = \min \{K \in \mathbb{N} : \Omega(K) \ge W_0\}$.}

\textbf{Proof:} At grid depth $K$, price has reached $P_K = P_0 - K \delta$. The $j$-th position (opened at $P_j = P_0 - j \delta$ with volume $V_0 \gamma^j$) suffers an adverse price move of $(K - j)\delta$. Multiplying by contract size $M_c$ and summing over all $j \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ yields Eq.~(6). For maintenance margin, summing required margin $m V_0 \gamma^j$ over all $K+1$ open positions yields the geometric series in Eq.~(7). Both terms are strictly positive and monotonically increasing in $K$. 

For an arithmetic grid ($\gamma = 1$), $L(K) = M_c \delta V_0 \frac{K(K+1)}{2} \in \mathcal{O}(K^2)$ and $M_{req}(K) = m V_0 (K+1) \in \mathcal{O}(K)$. For a geometric grid ($\gamma > 1$), both $L(K)$ and $M_{req}(K)$ grow exponentially as $\mathcal{O}(\gamma^K)$. Because the price process $P_t$ is a non-degenerate semimartingale with volatility $\sigma > 0$, the maximum excursion $\sup_{0 \le s \le t} |P_s - P_0|$ diverges almost surely as $t \to \infty$. Thus, the critical depth $K^* = \min \{K : \Omega(K) \ge W_0\}$ is reached in finite time almost surely, triggering catastrophic margin liquidation. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Doob's Optional Stopping Theorem and Supermartingale Wealth}
Under positive execution frictions ($C_{rt} > 0, S_t > 0$), the discrete account wealth process $\{W_t\}_{t \ge 0}$ under zero technical alpha is a strict supermartingale with respect to the filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[W_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = W_t - \mathbb{E}[\text{Frictions}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] < W_t.
\end{equation}
By Doob's Optional Stopping Theorem, for any stopping time $\tau$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}[\tau] < \infty$ and uniformly integrable stopped process $W_{t \wedge \tau}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[W_{\tau}] \le W_0 - \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{s=1}^{\tau} \text{Frictions}_s \right] < W_0.
\end{equation}
Hence, no order rescheduling, dynamic position averaging, or martingale multiplier can transform a negative-expectation indicator into a positive-expectation trading strategy. The apparent profitability observed in retail backtests is solely a consequence of right-censored stopping times.

\section{Benchmark Methodology and Empirical Audit Framework}
To ensure complete reproducibility and eliminate zero-friction simulation biases, all strategy evaluations adhered to the \textbf{Berrytrade Institutional Audit Protocol v1.0}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution Tick Simulation:} Executed on MetaTrader 5 (builds 4040/6230) utilizing genuine tick sequences (``Every tick based on real ticks'').
    \item \textbf{Microstructure Friction Calibration:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Broker Commissions:} Fixed at \$7.00 USD per round-turn standard lot (\$3.50 per side).
        \item \textbf{Floating Spreads:} Modeled directly on interbank variable spreads (Gold: 20--30 points during active European/US sessions, widening to 50--100 points during the daily 21:00--23:00 GMT rollover).
        \item \textbf{Latency:} 120\,ms network matching and order transit delay.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Account Parameters:} Initial equity \$10,000 USD, leverage 1:100, account base currency USD, broker margin call threshold at 50\%, stop-out forced liquidation threshold at 20\%.
    \item \textbf{Data Provenance Separation:} To address ambiguities in retail marketing, we strictly delineate between:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Author-Reported Baselines:} Historical backtest runs provided by original repository authors over heterogeneous historical windows (2019--2023).
        \item \textit{Standardized Multi-Year Audit:} Uniform 3-year continuous stress testing (2023.01.01--2025.12.31) capturing the 2024 gold trend expansion.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Paired Four-Arm Ablation Study: Isolating Indicator Alpha from Grid Mechanics}
To rigorously evaluate whether technical indicator signals contribute predictive power or whether profitability is purely manufactured by the grid mechanism, we designed a controlled, four-arm paired ablation experiment. All four experimental arms were executed on identical historical gold quotes (\texttt{XAUUSD}) spanning five years (2021--2025), partitioned into an \textbf{In-Sample window} (2021.01.01--2023.12.31) and an \textbf{Out-of-Sample window} (2024.01.01--2025.12.31), under identical \$7.00/lot commissions and floating spreads.

The four experimental arms are defined as:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Arm 1 (Raw Indicator Signal):} Standalone technical entry signals (Bollinger Bands + RSI extremes) executed with fixed lot size (0.10 lots) and a standard hard stop-loss (150 points). No grid averaging.
    \item \textbf{Arm 2 (Raw Signal + Unhedged Grid Overlay):} Identical indicator entry signals coupled with the original geometric grid averaging overlay (spacing 20 points, geometric volume multiplier).
    \item \textbf{Arm 3 (Raw Signal + Disciplined Volatility Risk Sizing):} Identical indicator signals executed with institutional risk management: position size dynamically scaled to risk exactly 1.0\% of account equity, ATR-based trailing stop-loss, and absolute prohibition of grid averaging.
    \item \textbf{Arm 4 (Pseudo-Random Entry + Grid Overlay):} Indicator signals replaced entirely with a pseudo-random coin-toss generator (50\% probability long/short), coupled with the identical unhedged grid overlay from Arm 2.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Multiple Testing Correction}
In empirical backtesting, running multiple strategy trials induces severe selection bias \cite{bailey2014deflated, harvey2016and}. To correct for selection bias, non-normality, and sample length, we compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) following Bailey and L{\'o}pez de Prado \cite{bailey2014deflated}. 

Let $\widehat{SR}$ denote the annualized sample Sharpe ratio calculated from $T$ daily return observations, with sample skewness $\hat{\gamma}_3$ and sample kurtosis $\hat{\gamma}_4$. Under the null hypothesis $H_0: SR \le SR^*$, the expected maximum Sharpe ratio across $N$ tested strategy trials is derived by Bailey and L{\'o}pez de Prado \cite{bailey2014deflated} as:
\begin{equation}
SR^* = \sqrt{\mathbb{V}[\{\widehat{SR}_k\}]} \left( (1 - \gamma_E) \Phi^{-1}\left(1 - \frac{1}{N}\right) + \gamma_E \Phi^{-1}\left(1 - \frac{1}{N e}\right) \right),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_E \approx 0.5772156649$ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and $\mathbb{V}[\{\widehat{SR}_k\}]$ is the variance of Sharpe ratio estimates across the trial space. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio is then given by:
\begin{equation}
DSR = \Phi\left( \frac{(\widehat{SR} - SR^*) \sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt{1 - \hat{\gamma}_3 \widehat{SR} + \frac{\hat{\gamma}_4 - 1}{4} \widehat{SR}^2}} \right).
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:dsr} presents the complete statistical inputs and resulting test statistics computed directly from authentic daily equity records. Across the eleven evaluated strategies, cross-trial variance is $\mathbb{V}[\widehat{SR}] = 4.203$ ($N = 11$), yielding an empirical benchmark threshold of $SR^* = 3.33$.

\section{Practical Governance: The Berrytrade Two-Tier Framework}
To protect open-source quantitative practitioners from catastrophic risk, the \textbf{Berrytrade-EA} ecosystem institutes an automated two-tier funnel:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Experimental Tier (Incubation):} Allows community contributions for algorithmic exploration, but mandates strict metadata declaration (\texttt{uses\_grid: true}, \texttt{risk\_level: "Extreme"}).
    \item \textbf{Verified Tier (Production-Grade):} Imposes non-negotiable hurdles: 3+ years tick data, maximum equity drawdown $\le 20\%$, profit factor $\ge 1.35$, mandatory hard stop-loss, and absolute prohibition of unhedged martingale/grid mechanics.
    \item \textbf{Automated Continuous Integration:} GitHub Actions pipelines scan every PR for forbidden native Win32 DLL calls (\texttt{user32.dll}, \texttt{kernel32.dll}) and undeclared telemetry, ensuring complete code transparency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Limitations}
Several empirical and methodological limitations must be acknowledged:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Convenience Sample Scope:} The cross-section analyzed consists of nineteen open-source Expert Advisors from retail repositories. While representative of retail MQL5 trading bot architectures, our empirical findings cannot be generalized to institutional high-frequency market-making or statistical arbitrage strategies.
    \item \textbf{Execution Latency Approximations:} While our benchmark models 120\,ms transit delay and interbank floating spreads, real-world execution on retail broker bridges can experience non-linear slippage, asymmetrical requotes, and broker-specific execution queues during macroeconomic news releases.
    \item \textbf{Parameter Optimization Bounds:} The strategies evaluated reflect parameter sets published by original developers. While walk-forward optimization may yield parameter subsets with enhanced short-term stability, Theorem 1 proves that no parameter tuning can avert ruin if unhedged grid averaging is retained.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This study has provided an exhaustive empirical dissection of retail Expert Advisors, uncovering the systematic substitution of tail risk for true statistical alpha. The ubiquitous practice of superimposing grid overlays onto unprofitable indicator signals creates a deceptive facade of steady gains that inevitably terminates in account liquidation during sustained trending regimes.

Through open-source dissemination, standardized benchmarking, and transparent peer review, the \textbf{Berrytrade-EA} project aims to elevate retail algorithmic trading to institutional standards of scientific reproducibility, risk discipline, and capital preservation.
